# The Studio

## In short

- The **Studio** keeps a performance as audio or as a video. It is the one place to download audio.
- It opens over Lumiverse in a workspace of its own, with the preview large and the settings beside it. See [The workspace](#the-workspace).
- It holds the last message you played, or one opened from **Saved audio** with **Open in the Studio**.
- **Saved audio**, one of its parts, is a gallery of everything saved, with names, folders, find and sort. See [Saved audio](getting-started.md#saved-audio).
- **Saved looks** keeps a video's look under a name, to use again.
- You choose which parts go in, where it starts and ends, a pause between parts, and how loud they are.
- **Audio** saves one WAV file, or a ZIP with a WAV for each part.
- **Video** makes a video that looks like a story being read. You pick its shape, font, colors, background, transition between lines, title and ending, and add notes for whoever watches.
- **Hide and bleep words** covers words on screen and bleeps them in the audio, such as a name you do not want to share.
- A preview shows the video with your choices. Drag its bar to any moment, or play it.
- Nothing is sent anywhere. Everything is made on your device from the audio you already heard, so it costs nothing.

## Steps

1. Play a message, on the Stage tab or with the button on a message. For an older message, press **Open in the Studio** on it under **Saved audio** instead.
2. Open the Studio: press **Open the Studio** on the **Studio** tab in the drawer. **Download or make a video**, under **Now playing** on the Stage tab, opens it too.
3. Under **The performance and scenes**, open **Which parts go in** and switch off any part you do not want. To cut the start or the end, use **Trim**.
4. For audio, pick **One file** or **A file for each part** under **Audio**, then press **Download audio** at the top.
5. For a video, set the look in the parts of the Studio, such as **Shape and words** and **Background**. Watch the preview change.
6. Press **Make a video** at the top. It is saved when it ends.

## What it sends

Nothing. The audio, the ZIP and the video are made in your browser, from the audio already heard.

Some things are kept on your account in files of their own: a picture for each background that has one, at most 1920 pixels on its long side, your own fonts, and the pictures for moods. See [Privacy](privacy.md).

## An example

Corvin Ashby keeps a lighthouse in a made-up story. A message has three parts:

1. Narration: "The storm hit the glass all night."
2. A sound: thunder.
3. Corvin says: "Pass me the lamp, Ilse. Ilse's hands are steadier than mine."

The reader wants to share a video, without the name Ilse.

1. They open the Studio.
2. Under **Hide and bleep words**, they switch on **Hide words** and type `Ilse` in **Words to hide**.
3. Under **Shape and words**, they pick **Tall, 9 by 16, for phones**.
4. They press **Make a video**.

The video:

- Opens with the chat's name for two seconds, then shows the narration.
- Shows `[thunder]` as a caption while the thunder plays.
- Shows Corvin's name above his line, which is in quotation marks. If he has a picture in the Cast tab, it shows above his name.
- Covers "Ilse." and "Ilse's" with a black bar. The final 's and the period are matched too.
- Bleeps both words in the sound.
- Fades to black at the end.

The file is saved as an MP4 or a WebM, named with the chat's name and the day.

## The workspace

### In short

- The Studio opens over Lumiverse, in a workspace of its own.
- With the **App manipulation** permission, it covers the whole screen. Without it, it opens in Lumiverse's extension window, as large as Lumiverse allows, and says how to get full screen.
- On a laptop, the parts of the Studio are listed on the left, the preview is in the middle, and the settings of the part picked are on the right. **Hide the list** makes more room for the preview.
- On a phone, the preview is at the top, and the settings are under it. **Part of the Studio** picks which settings show. The settings scroll, and the preview stays in view.
- Under the preview, the parts of the performance are shown as blocks along the video. A press on one goes to it.
- Every change is saved as you make it, so closing the Studio loses nothing.
- On a phone, the top bar keeps **Make a video**, **Download audio**, **Undo** and **Close**. **More**, the three dots, holds **Redo**, **Lock all** and **Focus**. On a laptop, they are all in the bar.

### Steps

1. Open it with **Open the Studio** on the drawer's **Studio** tab. These open it too:
   - **Open the Studio** in the chat's **Extras** menu, while **In the Extras menu** is on.
   - **Open the Studio** in the floating player's menu.
   - **Download or make a video** on the Stage tab, and **Open in the Studio** under **Saved audio**.
   - Searching for Auto Foley, the Studio or a video in Lumiverse's command palette (Ctrl+K or Cmd+K) opens the drawer's **Studio** tab, with its **Open the Studio** button.
   Opening the Studio closes Lumiverse's drawer, so the Studio is in view. While the Studio is open, the drawer's **Studio** tab says "The Studio is open." with **Go to the Studio**, which closes the drawer again.
2. Pick a part of the Studio and change its settings. The preview changes at once.
3. Close it with **Close** at the top, or Escape.

#### The timeline

Under the preview, the parts of the performance are blocks along the video.

- A tap on a block goes to the start of that part and picks it.
- **Cut it out** takes the parts picked out of the video and the audio, and what follows closes the gap. A thin red mark shows where a part was cut; a press on the mark puts it back. Cutting more than one part asks first.
- **Range starts here** and **Range ends here** pick every part between two moments of the bar, for cutting a run of parts.
- A handle at the start and the end of the sound: drag the start one to cut the beginning, the end one to cut the end. The preview shows the frame at the handle while it moves. **Clear the trim** keeps it all again, and **Trim**, under **The performance and scenes**, has the same in seconds.
- Cuts and the trim change only the video and the downloads, never the saved audio. Cuts are kept with the performance in **Saved audio**.

**Undo** and **Redo**, at the top, take back a change to the Studio's settings and make it again. Deleting a background with a picture, a watermark with a picture, or a sticker, deletes the picture too, so Undo goes back no further than that.

### What it sends

Nothing. The workspace is drawn on your device.

### An example

Tamsin edits a video on a laptop, with **App manipulation** granted.

1. She presses **Open the Studio**. The Studio covers the screen.
2. She picks **Background** on the left, and picks a shape on the preview.
3. She presses Ctrl and D to copy the shape, then Shift and Right four times to move the copy 40 pixels.
4. She changes her mind, and presses Ctrl and Z until the copy is gone.
5. She presses Escape. The Studio closes, and the drawer shows it as it was.

### Quick shortcuts

The drawer's **Studio** tab has **Open the Studio**, and under it **Quick shortcuts**, for a file in one tap without opening the Studio.

- **Download audio** saves the whole performance in one file, whatever **Save as** says, with any parts you cut or trimmed on the timeline left out. The Studio's own **Download audio** follows **Save as**.
- **Make a video** uses the look in the Studio's preview, with every setting, and the cuts you made on the timeline. It is the same video the Studio makes. The line under it counts the time, and **Stop the video** stops it.
- Both use the performance that is loaded. With nothing loaded, they use the newest saved performance in the open chat, and the line above them says which, such as "For: the latest performance in this chat, 3 minutes ago". **Pick another from Saved audio** opens the Studio on **Saved audio**.
- With nothing saved in the chat, or no chat open, they wait, and the line says to play a message first.
- To change the look first, open the Studio.

### Keyboard shortcuts

On a computer, these keys work while the Studio is open and you are not typing in a box. On a Mac, use Cmd in place of Ctrl.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Space | Play or stop the preview |
| Left and Right | Back or forward one second |
| Shift with Left and Right | The part before or after |
| Home and End | The start or the end |
| F | The preview full screen |
| Ctrl and Z | Undo |
| Ctrl, Shift and Z, or Ctrl and Y | Redo |
| Delete or Backspace | Delete the shape picked, after asking, or cut the parts picked out |
| I and O | A range starts or ends where the bar is |
| Ctrl and D | Copy the shape picked |
| Ctrl and ] or [ | Bring the shape picked forward, or send it back |
| Arrow keys, with the preview pressed | Move the shape picked 1 pixel, or 10 with Shift |
| Ctrl and S | Say that everything is saved |
| ? | The list of keys |
| Escape | Close a pop-up, then full screen, then the Studio |

- **Keyboard shortcuts**, a part of the Studio, turns them off if a key clashes with something else you use. Every action also has a button. Escape still closes the Studio.
- **Keyboard shortcuts** at the top opens the same list. On a computer with a mouse, each button's hover text says its key.
- Lumiverse's own keys, such as Ctrl and K for the command palette, always go on to Lumiverse.
- A key is only read inside the Studio. A key in another extension's pop-up, or one another extension or Lumiverse has already used, is left alone.

### Details

- Opened again, the Studio shows the part it showed last.
- If **App manipulation** is taken away while the Studio is open, it opens again in Lumiverse's window, on the same part. If it is granted while the Studio is open in the window, the next time it opens it covers the screen.
- The workspace lays itself out by its own size, so it fits the window on a tablet, a phone turned sideways and a small laptop the same way.
- Reduce motion, in the panel or on your device, turns off its fade and slide.
- Only the settings and the list of parts scroll, and the preview's column when the settings sit beside it. None of them keeps room for a scroll bar. A thin bar shows over the edge while you scroll, then fades.

## Projects

### In short

- A project saves how a video is made. It never saves the video file.
- It keeps the saved performance it uses, the trim, the cuts and scenes you made on the timeline, and every Studio setting.
- Pictures and fonts are kept by their ids, so a project does not copy them.
- While a project is open, each change is saved to it on its own, a moment after you stop.
- Projects are listed in the Studio under **Projects**, with folders.

### Steps

1. Open the Studio and pick **Projects**.
2. Press **New project**. It starts from what the Studio shows now: the look and the performance. Its name shows at the top of the Studio.
3. Change the look, trim or cut parts. The top says **Project, saving**, then **Project, saved**.
4. To come back to it, press **Open** beside it. Every setting it holds is put back, so the preview shows the same frame.
5. **More** beside a project has its name, **Folder**, **Duplicate** and **Delete**. **Delete** asks first.
6. **Close the project** stops saving changes to it. The Studio keeps its look.

Opening another project saves the one open first.

### What it sends

Nothing leaves your device but the project itself, which is saved to your account with Lumiverse. No model is asked.

### An example

1. Tamsin plays a message from Orrin and opens the Studio. She presses **New project**. It is named **Project 1**.
2. She uses the **Paper page** template. A moment later the top says **Project, saved**.
3. She presses **New project** again for a second version. **Project 2** starts from the Paper page look, and she uses **Night sky** on it.
4. She presses **Open** beside **Project 1**. Project 2 is saved first. The Paper page look comes back, and the preview shows the same frame as before.
5. Under **More**, she types **Orrin at the mill** as its name. The top of the Studio follows.
6. Later she deletes the saved audio of Orrin's message. Opening **Orrin at the mill** shows: "The saved audio this project uses has been deleted. The video cannot be made until the project is linked to a saved performance. Everything else in it is open." She presses **Use another saved performance**, picks one, and presses **Use it**.

### Details

- A project's saved performance is the one open when it was made. Opening another saved performance in the Studio while a project is open makes the project use that one.
- A project whose saved audio is gone opens with that notice. Its look still opens.
- A save that fails warns once, in plain words, and the top says **Project, not saved**. The change stays on the page until it is saved.
- A background or watermark picture that a project uses stays on your account, even after it leaves the backgrounds in use.
- Up to 100 projects and 30 folders. A project is at most about 400 KB, which shapes and notes make up most of.
- A setup file does not carry projects or their folders, since they point at saved audio and pictures that the file does not carry either.
- **Start again** with **Settings** ticked deletes every project.

## Older performances

The Studio holds the last message you played since the page was opened. A message played before that is in **Saved audio**, in the Studio, while **Keep made audio** is on.

1. Find the message under **Saved audio**.
2. Press **Open in the Studio**.

The whole performance is built from what is saved, in order:

- Each line comes from **Keep made audio**.
- Each sound comes from your sound library.
- Nothing is spoken again, found, made or directed, so nothing is spent.
- A line with no saved audio, or a sound no longer in your library, is left out, and the Studio says how many.
- The title uses the name of whoever the script belongs to. The file name uses the name you gave it in **Saved audio**, or whose script it is, and the day it was saved.

Playing the message again works too. Lines played from kept audio spend nothing, but a sound not in your library may be found or made again.

## The performance

These choices change both the audio and the video. The first two are under **The performance and scenes**, and the rest under **Sound effects and music**.

- **Which parts go in**: a switch for each part heard, with **All** and **None**. It is for this performance only. Playing another message starts with every part in.
- **Trim**: **Start at** and **End at**, in seconds of the whole performance. 0 for **End at** is the end. The line under them says how long the whole performance is and what is kept. **Start here** and **End here**, under the preview, set them from where the preview is, and **Clear the trim** keeps it all again. The trim is for this performance only. The new ends fade over 10 thousandths of a second, so they do not click.
- **Sound effects**: on by default. Off, only the voices are kept.
- **Music**: on by default. It keeps the scene music that played. Off, no music is kept.
- **Pause between parts, in seconds**: 0 by default. A short pause, such as 0.3, adds a silence between lines, as in an audiobook.
- **As you heard it**: on by default. It uses your **Voices** and **Sounds** volumes and **Even voice levels**. Off, each part is as the voice service sent it.
- **Even out the loudness**: on by default. The loudest moment is set just under the most a file can hold, so the file is neither too quiet nor clipped.

## Audio

- **Download audio**, at the top of the Studio, saves the whole performance, with the parts and trim chosen.
- **Save as**: **One file** by default. **A file for each part** saves a ZIP, with the parts numbered in order and named by who says them. With **Show names** off, they are named "line", "narration" or "sound".
- **Download this part**, under **Audio**, saves the part playing now, exactly as the voice service sent it. A part with a hidden word in it is saved bleeped, as a WAV.

The audio is a 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV file. WAV keeps the sound exactly as it was. Each part fades in and out over 3 thousandths of a second, too short to hear, so two parts never meet with a click.

### Getting an MP3

Any audio converter can save the WAV as an MP3.

- **On a computer:** most audio programs can open a WAV and save it as an MP3.
- **On a phone:** the free [audio converter from Wave](https://wave.co/tools/audio-converter) works in the browser. Its page says the file is not uploaded. Open it in your phone's own browser, not inside the Lumiverse app, since an app may not let the page pick a file.

**Audio**, in the Studio, links to this section.

### Better sound from the start

The sound can only be as good as what the voice service sends.

- Each TTS connection in Lumiverse has an **output format**. Pick a higher one there, such as MP3 at 192 kbps or PCM at 44.1 kHz on ElevenLabs, or WAV on Fish Audio. Some formats need a paid plan with the service.
- A line kept in **Keep made audio** is played as it was first made. Press **Clear kept audio** to make lines again in a new format.

## Video

The preview draws the video with your choices. **Where** moves through it. **Play the preview** plays it with its sound, from where the bar is.

**Full screen** shows the preview over the whole page, in its own shape, with nothing else in view. It fades in and out. It has its own play button, bar and close button. Press **Close full screen** or Escape to go back. It does not switch your phone or browser into its own full screen, since that switch is a sudden cut to black. On another tab or app, the preview stops where it is, and **Play the preview** goes on from there.

**Make a video**, at the top of the Studio, records it as it plays, silently, so it takes as long as the performance and a few seconds more.

- The line under the buttons counts the time.
- On another tab or app, the video pauses, and it goes on when you come back.
- Some phones start the sound again only after a press. Then the video waits, and **Play the preview** becomes **Go on with the video**. Press it to carry on.
- **Stop the video** stops it and saves nothing.

A browser that can make MP4, such as a recent Chrome or Safari, saves MP4, which phones and sharing sites play. Any other browser saves WebM. The video is made at a high quality.

### The example clip

With no performance in the Studio, the preview shows an example clip, about 25 seconds long, so you can see the look and change it before playing anything.

- It has no sound. **Play the preview** plays it silently, and it starts again at the end until you stop it.
- It shows each part of the look, and each kind of change between parts: the title, narration, two sound captions in a row, the same person twice, two made-up people taking turns, a third who speaks after them, a line with a voice tag for a mood picture, quotation marks, a second scene, and the fade at the end. Notes and hidden words show when you have them.
- Its people, Juniper, Ansel and Bram, have pictures drawn by Auto Foley: a darker tint of their kind's color with their initial, so the ring in the full color stands out. The mood picture is a lighter tint. It never uses a picture from your cast.
- Its second scene is a calm morning in the shop. It takes a background the same way a real scene does: one whose **Use it for** has the mood calm or a place word such as morning or shop, or else the one for every other scene.
- Every change you make redraws it at once, at the same moment in the clip.
- With a performance there, **Show in the preview** picks **Your performance** or **Example**.
- The example is never saved. **Make a video** and the downloads use your performance, and stay off while there is none.

### Templates

**Templates**, a part of the Studio, has built-in looks. Each one sets the whole look: the fonts, colors and fills, shadows, the background or a design of shapes, how the words show, the transition, the pictures and the sizes. Your line under the title, watermarks, pictures, fonts you added, the title's words, notes and the video's shape stay as they are.

1. Press a template. The preview shows it at once.
2. Press another to compare, or **Go back to my look**.
3. Press **Use this template** to keep it. If your look now is not saved under **Saved looks**, it asks first, and saves your look as "My look before" the template's name. **Undo** puts your look back in one step.

Leaving **Templates** while you try one puts your look back.

### Shape and words

- **Shape**: **Wide, 16 by 9** by default, 1920 by 1080. **Tall** is 1080 by 1920, for phone videos such as Reels and Shorts. **Square** is 1080 by 1080.
- **Font**: **Book** by default. Also **Clean**, **Typewriter**, **Rounded** and **Your theme's font**. A font your device does not have is drawn in one that is close. Your own fonts are listed under **Your fonts**. See [Your own fonts](#your-own-fonts).
- **Size of the words, in percent**: 100 by default, from 60 to 160.
- **Color of the words**: a warm white by default.
- **Fill of the words**: **One color** by default. See [Gradients](#gradients).
- **How the words show**: **Fade in as they are said** by default, which fades each word in as it is said. **Light up as they are said** shows the words not said yet dimmer. **Appear as they are said** shows each word only once it is said. **Blur in as they are said** fades each word in from a blur. **All at once** shows the whole line.
- **Where the words sit**: **Middle** by default, or **Lower third**, which leaves the picture behind them clear.
- **Quotation marks around speech**: on by default. Narration never has them.
- **A shadow behind the words**: on by default, the soft dark shadow the words always had. Sound captions have it too. Under it: **Color of the shadow**, black by default, and **How dark** (65%), **How soft** (18) and **How far below** (2), set by eye on the preview. Sizes are in pixels of a 1080-pixel frame.

When each word is said is worked out from where the voice is in the clip, so it is close, not exact.
- **A box behind the words**: off by default. A box of one color behind the lines, which helps on a busy background. Under it: **Color of the box**, black by default; **How see-through, in percent**, 40 by default, where 0 is solid; **Rounded corners, in pixels**, 16 by default; and **How wide**, **To the words** or **Across the frame**.

### Speakers

- **Show names**: on by default. Off, no names are in the video, in its title, or in the name of any file saved, audio included. Names are still said aloud. To take a name out of the sound too, use **Hide and bleep words**. Under it:
  - **Font of the names**: **Clean** by default, beside the words in **Book**. Any font, your own included.
  - **Size of the names, in percent**: 100 by default, from 60 to 160. It is set apart from the size of the words and the size of the pictures.
  - **A shadow behind the names**: on by default, the same as the words' shadow, with its own color, darkness, softness and distance.
- **Show pictures**: on by default. Each speaker's picture from the Cast tab is shown above their line. Narration has none. Under it:
  - **Shape of the pictures**: a circle or a rounded square.
  - **Size of the pictures**: 150 by default, in pixels of a 1080-pixel frame.
  - **A ring around each picture**: on by default, in the color of the name. Off shows the picture with no ring. Under it, **How thick the ring is**: 3 by default, from 1 to 20, in pixels of a 1080-pixel frame. It is the same for every picture, two speakers and mood pictures too.
  - **Two speakers on screen**: on by default. See [Two speakers and mood pictures](#two-speakers-and-mood-pictures).
  - **A picture for each mood**: on by default. See [Two speakers and mood pictures](#two-speakers-and-mood-pictures).
- **Color of the names**: **Each kind's color** by default, the colors set under **Colors for each kind of part** in the Settings tab. **The words' color** uses one color for every name.
- **Fill of the names**: **One color** by default, the color above. See [Gradients](#gradients).

**Colors of their own in the video**, at the end of **Speakers**, is off by default. On, the rings and names in the video use their own colors, and the Stage tab, the chat and the players keep the general ones from **Colors for each kind of part**.

- A color for each kind: the narrator, characters, personas and NPCs. Each starts as the general color. **Use the general color** puts it back.
- **A color for one person**: pick someone from the cast and press **Give them a color**. Their ring and name in the video use it in place of their kind's. **Remove** takes it away. It is kept by the person, so renaming them keeps it.
- The timeline's blocks keep the general colors.
- The kind colors are part of a saved look, and a template can set them. A person's color is kept in projects, and a template never changes it.

### Background

- **A color of your own** by default: a soft dark blue gradient, from the middle out.
- **Dark**, lit a little in your theme's accent.
- **A color of your own**, picked under it, one color or a gradient.
- **A picture of your own**. **Choose a picture** keeps it on your account. It is cut to the video's shape from the middle. **Dim the picture**, 40% by default, makes the words easier to read. **Blur the picture**, off by default, makes a busy picture easier to read words over.
  - **A slow move on the picture**: on by default. In each scene the picture makes one slow move, easing in and out: it zooms in, moves right, zooms out or moves left, in turn. It moves 1% of the frame a second on average, and never more than 2%, so it looks still at a glance. With Reduce motion on, in the panel or on your device, it holds still in the preview and full screen. A saved video keeps the move unless this is off.
- **A design of your own**: shapes you place yourself, over a color. See [A design of your own](#a-design-of-your-own).

**Darken the edges**, under any background, makes the edges of the frame darker than the middle. It is 35% by default. **Off** leaves the edges as bright as the middle.

**Color of the edges**, under it, is black by default. Another color tints the edges with it, as strongly as **Darken the edges** says.

### Gradients

The words, the names, the sound captions, the line under the title, the text of each watermark, **A color of your own** and each shape in a design have a **Fill**:

- **One color**, the default: the color row above, with no gradient.
- **Linear gradient**, which runs in a **Direction**: top to bottom, left to right, or either diagonal.
- **Radial gradient**, which runs from the middle out to the corners.

How it works:

- Color 1 is the color row above the fill, such as **Color of the words**.
- Colors 2 to 6 are under the fill. **Add a color** adds one, up to six in all. **Remove**, on each color past the second, takes it out with no question, since it is quick to add back.
- The colors are spread evenly. There is no box for where each one sits, so the rows stay short on a phone.
- On text, the gradient runs across each whole line, caption or name, so every word reads the same way. The words effects, the covers on hidden words and the shadows work on top of it.
- Two very different colors can be harder to read. Check the preview.

A fill is part of your settings, a saved look, and each background in the list.

### Moving between lines

**Transition** sets how the screen changes from one line to the next. None of them flashes.

| Transition | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Fade and rise** | The default. The line fades out as it moves up a little, while the next fades in from a little below, at the same time. |
| **Fade out, then in** | The line fades out, then the next fades in, with no movement. The two overlap, so no frame is empty: half way, the screen is darker, not black. |
| **Dissolve** | The two lines fade into each other. |
| **Through black** | The whole frame fades to black and back. |
| **Slide across** | The line slides out to the left as the next slides in from the right. One fades as the other comes in, so the screen does not go dark between them. |
| **Soft wipe** | A soft edge moves across, taking the line away and showing the next. |
| **Soft focus** | The line goes soft as it fades, and the next comes into focus. |
| **Gentle zoom** | The two lines fade into each other, with a small zoom. |
| **Cut, with no movement** | The next line shows at once. |

**How long it takes, in seconds**: 0.5 by default, from 0.1 to 2.

The title gives way to the first line with the same transition.

The pictures move on their own during a transition. A person on screen in both lines moves from their old place to their new one, such as from the middle to the left when a second person comes in. A person who arrives fades in, and one who leaves fades out. The words and names use the transition picked. With **Cut, with no movement**, the pictures change at once. With **Through black**, they go through black with the rest.

### Animations

**Animations** picks how each part of the video comes in and goes out: the opening, the ending, between lines, a scene change, its background, pictures, lines, words, the title, captions and watermarks. See [Animations](animations.md).

### Title and ending

- **Title at the start**: on by default. The title shows for two seconds before the first line.
- **Title**, under it: empty uses the chat's name, while **Show names** is on. A title you type is always shown.
- **Fade to black at the end, in seconds**: 1.5 by default. 0 ends at once.

### Sounds

- **Show sounds as captions**: on by default. A sound shows as a caption in square brackets, such as [thunder]. Off, the background shows alone while it plays.
- **Color of the captions**, under it: the color of the words by default. Pick another, such as yellow, to set captions apart from the lines.

### Notes

A note is a few words for whoever watches, in a dark box at the top or the bottom of the video. It fades in and out.

- **Add a note** adds one for the whole video. **Add a note here** adds one that starts where the preview is.
- **When it shows**: **The whole video**, **At the start** or **At the end**, which show for 4 seconds, or **From a time**, with **From** and **For how long** in seconds.
- **Where**: **Top** or **Bottom**. Two notes in the same place at the same time are stacked.
- Up to 10 notes, each up to 300 characters. **Remove this note** takes one out.
- Notes are saved to your account, so they can be used on the next video.
- Hidden words are covered in notes too.

### Saved looks

**Saved looks** keeps the look of a video under a name, to use again.

- **Save this look**: type a name, then press it. The look holds the shape, words, captions, pictures, edges, transition and ending, and one background: the one in use, with its design and settings.
- **Use** puts a saved look back. If it is already the look in use, it says so.
- The look's background goes into your list of backgrounds, in place of the one with the same id, or added if there is none, and is used for every scene nothing else is for. Every other background, with its design and **Use it for**, stays.
- A look saved with Auto Foley 1.9.0 or older has its one background, which becomes that one entry.
- Type over a look's name to rename it. **Delete** asks first.
- Notes, the title you typed, **Show names** and **Show pictures** are not part of a look, so using one never changes them.
- A look with **A picture of your own** uses the picture that background has now. A look keeps the background's settings, not its picture.
- Up to 20 looks, saved to your account.

### Back to the built-in look

**Back to the built-in look**, at the top of **Templates** and under **Saved looks**, puts back every setting of the look, exactly as the **The built-in look** template sets it. It asks first. If your look now is not saved under **Saved looks**, it offers to save it first, as **Use this template** does. Your shape of video, watermarks, the words of the line under the title, notes, pictures, fonts and projects stay. **Undo** takes it back in one step. If nothing would change, it says so and asks nothing.

### Start again

Every choice in the Studio is a setting, kept on your account. **Start again** in the Settings tab, with **Settings** ticked, puts them all back: the look, the notes, the words to hide, the background pictures, your own fonts and your projects. A setup file carries the settings too, but not the background pictures, your fonts or the pictures for moods.

## Your own fonts

- **Add a font**, under **Font** or **Font of the names**, adds a font file from your device: TTF, OTF, WOFF or WOFF2.
- Use a font you own, or a free one. Free fonts are on font sites, which say what each font allows.
- Check the font's license. It has to allow use in videos you post, such as on a sharing site. Some free fonts allow personal use only.
- Once it is added, its name is in the list, and a line of words under it is drawn in it, so you can see it worked.
- It is kept on your account, so it is there on every device you sign in on.
- It is named from its file. `Harbor_Script.woff2` is listed as **Harbor Script**, under **Your fonts**.
- A font can be up to 3 MB, since it travels through the extension. A WOFF2 file is the smallest kind.
- You can keep up to 8 fonts.
- **Take this font out** shows while one of your fonts is picked. It asks first. A video that used it is drawn in **Book**.
- If a font cannot be read, the video is drawn in **Book**, and the Log says why.

## A design of your own

### In short

- **A design of your own** is a background made of shapes: rectangles, rounded rectangles, ellipses, triangles, lines, text boxes, hearts, stars, polygons, diamonds, arrows, speech bubbles, rings, crescent moons, clouds, sparkles, cat, dog and bunny faces, paw prints, bones, fish, carrots, bats, roses, checkerboard strips, broken hearts, stripes, snowflakes, ornaments, holly, pumpkins, ghosts, autumn leaves, fireworks, lanterns, diya lamps, menorahs, shamrocks, eggs and flowers.
- The shapes stay where you put them for the whole video.
- You place them on the preview with a finger or the mouse, or type exact numbers.
- Each shape has its own color, an optional gradient, and how see-through it is.
- The design is part of your settings, and part of a saved look.

### Steps

1. Open **Background** in the Studio's **Video** card.
2. Set **Background** to **A design of your own**.
3. Pick the color behind the shapes under **Background color**.
4. Pick a kind under **Shape to add**, then press **Add the shape**. It is added in the middle and picked.
5. Change it:
   - Drag the shape on the preview to move it.
   - Drag the dot at its corner to change its size.
   - Drag the dot above it to turn it.
   - Or type in **Across, in percent**, **Down, in percent**, **Width**, **Height** and **Turn, in degrees**.
6. Set **Color**, **Fill of the shape** for a gradient, and **See-through**. See [Gradients](#gradients).
7. Press another shape in the list to change it. Press the picked one again to put it down.

**Duplicate** adds a copy just beside it. **Bring forward** and **Send back** move it in front of or behind the shape next to it. **Delete** asks first.

**Shape to add** is a grid of small pictures with their names. Some shapes have settings of their own:

- **Star**: **Points**, 5 by default, from 4 to 12, and **How deep the points are, in percent**, 50 by default.
- **Polygon**: **Sides**, 6 by default, from 3 to 12.
- **Speech bubble**: **Where the tail points**: down, up, left or right.
- **Ring**: **How thick, in percent**, 18 by default.

- **Cat face**, **Dog face** and **Bunny face**: **Color of the eyes**, dark brown by default, and **Pink cheeks**, on by default. A **Fish** has an eye, and a **Rose** a curl, in the same color.
- **Stripes**: bands of the colors under **Fill of the shape**, from top to bottom, up to seven. A gradient direction of right turns them into bands from left to right.

Every shape but a text box can have an outline: **Color of the outline**, white by default, and **Outline, in pixels**, 0 by default, which is none. **Flip across** and **Flip up and down** mirror a shape.

### What it sends

Nothing. The design is drawn on your device. It is saved with your settings, on your account.

### An example

Corin wants a night sky behind a story told on a pier.

1. **Background color** is `#101830`, a dark blue.
2. An **Ellipse** for the moon: **Across** 78, **Down** 22, **Width** 160, **Height** 160, **Color** `#f2e8c9`.
3. A **Rectangle** for the water: **Across** 50, **Down** 92, **Width** 4000, **Height** 240, **Color** `#1b2a48`, and **Fill of the shape** set to **Linear gradient**, top to bottom, with **Color 2** `#0a1020`, so it gets darker toward the bottom.
4. A **Text box** with the words **The Pier**: **Across** 50, **Down** 10, **Width** 600, **Height** 80, **See-through** 30%.

The moon is a circle in every shape of video, since sizes are in pixels of a 1080-pixel frame. In a tall video it is still near the top right, since places are shares of the frame. The water is wider than any frame, so it reaches both sides. Corin types 5000 in **Width** once: when the box is left, it shows 4000, the widest a shape can be.

### Details

- The shapes are drawn back to front, in the order of the list. The first is at the back.
- A design can have up to 40 shapes.
- **Across** and **Down** say where the middle of the shape is, from 0 to 100, with 50 in the middle. From -50 to 150 lets a shape hang past the edge.
- **Width** and **Height** are from 4 and 2 to 4000 pixels of a 1080-pixel frame. A line uses **Height** as its thickness.
- **Turn, in degrees** is from -180 to 180. 0 is level.
- A new shape is a muted purple, and a new text box a warm white. **Use the built-in color** puts that color back.
- A text box fills its box with its words, as large as fits, in the font of the words.
- **Darken the edges** is drawn over the design.
- The words, pictures and names are drawn over it.
- While the design is shown, a finger on the preview moves shapes and does not scroll the page. Scroll above or below the preview.
- The outline and the dots show on the preview only. They are never in full screen or in a saved video.

### Locks and focus

- **Lock**, in a shape's settings, keeps it where it is: it cannot be dragged, sized, turned, moved with the arrow keys or deleted. A press on it in the preview still picks it, so it can be unlocked. The list of shapes says **locked** after its name.
- **Lock all**, at the top of the Studio, locks every shape and sticker at once. While it is on, it reads **Locked** and its padlock is closed. On a phone, it is in **More**.
- **Lock the timeline**, beside **Cut it out**, takes the trim handles away and turns **Cut it out** and putting a part back off, so nothing is cut by accident.
- Locks are saved with the shapes, in looks and in projects. **Undo** takes back a lock.
- **Focus**, at the top (in **More** on a phone), hides the list of parts, the settings and the timeline, and the preview takes the space. Play, the bar for where you are and **Exit focus** stay. On a laptop they fade after three seconds without the mouse moving and come back when it moves. On a phone, a tap on the preview where there is no shape hides or shows them. Shapes can still be picked and moved. **\\** turns focus on and off, and Escape leaves it. The settings are where you left them.

### Snapping

While you drag, size or turn a shape on the preview, it snaps to helpful places and shows a thin line where it snapped:

- the middle of the frame, across and up and down, its edges, a margin of 5% inside them, and the thirds;
- the edges and middles of the other shapes, so shapes line up;
- the width or height of another shape, while sizing;
- 0, 45, 90, 135 and 180 degrees, while turning.

It reaches a few pixels, so it helps without holding the shape. **Snap while moving**, under the shape picked, is on by default; off, nothing snaps. On a computer, hold Alt, or Option on a Mac, while dragging to move it freely that once. **Center across** and **Center up and down** put the shape picked in the middle exactly. The lines show on the preview only, never in full screen or a saved video.

## Scenes and their backgrounds

### In short

- You can have more than one background, and each scene of a performance can have its own.
- The Director marks where a scene starts: a new place, a jump in time, or a mood that turns and stays turned. It does this in the call it already makes.
- Each background says which scenes it is for: some moods, some place words, or only scenes you pick it for.
- One background is for every scene nothing else is for.
- You can change any scene's background, start a new scene, or join one to the scene before, in the Studio.
- One background fades into the next over about a second. A video with one scene looks the same as with one background.

### Steps

1. Open **Background** in the Studio's **Video** card.
2. Press **Add a background**. Give it a **Name**, and set it up like any background.
3. Under **Use it for**, pick one:
   - **Every scene nothing else is for**. This is the default background. There is always one.
   - **Scenes with these moods or places**, then press the moods and type the place words, one per line.
   - **Only scenes I pick it for**.
4. Play a message. With two or more backgrounds, the Director names each scene's place and mood.
5. In the Studio, open **Scenes** under **The performance**. Each scene shows its first words, its place, its mood and its background.
6. To change a scene's background, pick one in its **Background** box. **Automatic** goes back to the one picked for you.
7. To split a scene, pick a line under **Start a new scene at** and press **Start a scene there**. To merge one, press **Join it to the scene before**.

**Background to change** picks which background the boxes change. While **Background** is open, the preview shows that background behind the whole video. Close it to see each scene's own.

### What it sends

- With two or more backgrounds, the Director's rules have `"scenes": true` and the list of moods, each with its name and what it is for. The list is **Scene moods**, on the Sounds tab. See [Changing the moods](music.md#changing-the-moods). It sends back a `"scene"`, a few words naming the place, on the line a scene starts, with a `"mood"`. See [The prompt](director.md#the-prompt).
- With one background, nothing more is sent, so nothing more is spent.
- Changes you make in **Scenes** are kept with the performance in **Saved audio**. They are not sent anywhere else.

### An example

Ilsabet has two backgrounds:

- **Hall**, a red color, for **Every scene nothing else is for**.
- **Dock**, a blue color, for **Scenes with these moods or places**, with the mood **Calm** and the place word `dock`.

She plays a message with three lines:

1. "The long hall was empty but for the lamps." The Director sends `"scene": "long hall"` and `"mood": "tense"`.
2. "We should not wait here much longer, the lamps are failing." No scene.
3. "By morning the ferry had reached the far dock and the water was still." The Director sends `"scene": "ferry dock"` and `"mood": "calm"`.

**Scenes** lists two scenes. The first is on **Hall**, since nothing else is for a tense long hall. The second is on **Dock**: its mood is calm, and its place has the word dock. In the video, the first two lines are on red. When the third line starts, the blue fades in over about a second.

She picks **Dock** for the first scene too. Now the whole video is blue, and reopening the performance from **Saved audio** keeps that.

### Details


- **Use backgrounds for scenes**, under the list of backgrounds, is on by default. Off, every scene uses the background for every other scene, and your other backgrounds and their **Use it for** stay as they are. A template turns it off, so the template shows the same in every scene. Turn it on to use your other backgrounds again.
- A background is picked for a scene in this order: one you picked by hand, then the first background in the list whose moods or place words match, then the one for every other scene.
- Place words match whole words in the Director's place, with capitals and punctuation left out. `dock` matches "ferry dock", and not "docker".
- A mood comes from the Director's scene mark, or else from the mood the music last had.
- Scenes in a row on the same background do not fade.
- A message directed with one background, or read as written, is one scene. Start a scene by hand, or press **Direct again** on the Stage tab once you have two backgrounds.
- A performance not kept in **Saved audio** keeps your scene changes for this visit only. The line under the scenes says so.
- You can have up to 12 backgrounds. Deleting one asks first, and its scenes go to the one for every other scene.

### How backgrounds are kept

Your backgrounds are kept as a list, each with an id, a name and its own settings. One is picked for every scene nothing else is for. A picture for a background is kept in a file of its own, named by the background's id.

## Two speakers and mood pictures

### In short

- **Two speakers on screen**: when two people take turns speaking in a scene, both pictures show side by side. The one speaking is at full strength, and the other is a little dimmer.
- **A picture for each mood**: a person can have a picture for each mood, on the Cast tab. The list of moods is yours to change. A line is shown with the picture for its mood.
- Both are in **Speakers**, under **Show pictures**, and on by default.
- Neither asks a model anything, so neither costs anything.
- With no mood pictures, and one person speaking at a time, there is one picture: the main picture of the person speaking.

### Steps

1. On the Cast tab, check the moods under **Moods for pictures**. Add your own, or change the words that pick one.
2. Open a person and open **Pictures for moods**, under **Picture**.
3. Press **Choose** beside a mood, and crop the picture the same way as the main one.
4. Play a message. In the Studio, each line of theirs shows the picture for its mood.

### What it sends

Nothing. The mood is worked out on your device, from the line. The pictures are kept on your account. See [Pictures for moods](cast.md#pictures-for-moods) for the moods, their words, and which wins a tie.

### An example

Brisa and Tollan are made-up characters, in one scene. Both have a picture, and Tollan has an **Angry** picture.

1. Brisa speaks. One picture shows: Brisa's.
2. Tollan answers: "[angrily] Give the map back." Both pictures now show, side by side: Brisa's moves from the middle to the left, and Tollan's fades in on the right. Tollan's is at full strength, and it is his **Angry** picture, since the tag says angrily. Brisa's is a little dimmer.
3. A line of narration. Both stay, a little dimmer.
4. Brisa speaks again. Her picture is at full strength, and Tollan's is dimmer.
5. Marek speaks, a third person. It goes back to one picture: Marek's.

### Details


- **How the quiet one looks**, under **Two speakers on screen**: **Fade**, by default, makes the one not speaking partly see-through, so it blends into the background. **Darken** darkens it. **Smaller** makes it a little smaller, with a light fade. **No change** leaves it as it is. **How strong, in percent** is 45 by default. The change from one speaker to the other follows the line's transition.
- Two people are shown when they are the last two to speak in the scene, and both have a picture. A new scene starts again from one.
- The first of the two to speak is on the left.
- Names show under each picture, cut short with an ellipsis if there is no room.
- When there is no room for two pictures side by side, such as a tall video with large pictures, one picture shows.
- Between one picture and two, a person on screen in both lines moves to their new place over the transition, and only the one who arrives or leaves fades. See [Moving between lines](#moving-between-lines).
- A line's mood comes from its voice tags and its note on how it is said, matched against the words of each mood. With none, it is the scene's mood. With no mood either, the main picture is used.

## Marking a video as yours

### In short

- **A line under the title** puts a short line, such as "by" and your name, under the title at the start of the video.
- **Watermarks**, up to five, put your name, a logo, or both on the video.
- A watermark sits in a corner, at the top or bottom in the middle, or in the middle under the words. It never covers the words.
- Each one can show for the whole video, at the start, at the end, from a time for some seconds, or only during scenes on some backgrounds.
- They are drawn in the preview, the example clip, full screen and every saved video. The downloaded audio has none.
- Both are empty or off by default, so a video has none until you add them.

### Steps

1. Open **Title and ending** in the Studio, and type in **A line under the title**. Pick **Color of the line** if you want it apart from the title.
2. Open **Watermarks** and press **Add a watermark**.
3. Type its **Text**, or press **Choose a picture** for a PNG, JPEG or WebP logo, or both.
4. Pick **Where**, **How big, in pixels** and **How strong**.
5. Pick **When**. For **From a time, for a number of seconds**, set the time and how long.
6. Look at the preview. Each change shows at once.

**Delete** takes a watermark out, and asks first. Its picture is taken out of your account with it.

### What it sends

Nothing. The line and the watermarks are drawn on your device. A watermark's picture is kept on your account in a file of its own. See [Privacy](privacy.md).

### An example

Wren Calloway posts her videos and wants them marked.

1. **A line under the title**: `by Wren Calloway`. The title card now reads the chat's name, then her line under it, smaller.
2. A watermark with her logo, a small round picture, and the text `Wren`. **Where**: **Bottom right**. **How big**: 64. **How strong**: 50%. **When**: **The whole video**.
3. A second watermark with the text `wrencalloway.example`. **Where**: **Top, in the middle**. **When**: **At the end**, so it shows for the last 4 seconds.

In the video, her logo and name sit in the bottom right corner the whole time, half see-through, clear of the words. Her address fades in at the top for the end.

### Details

- The line under the title is drawn only with a title. With **Title at the start** off, it is not shown.
- It has its own **Font of the line** (the words' font by default, your own fonts too), **Color of the line** (the title's color by default), **Fill of the line** (see [Gradients](#gradients)) and **A shadow behind the line**, which starts as the title's shadow.
- **How big** is the height of the watermark, from 12 to 400 pixels of a 1080-pixel frame. Its text is about two thirds of that.
- Its text has its own **Font of the text** (the words' font by default, your own fonts too), **Color of the text** (white by default), **Fill of the text** (see [Gradients](#gradients)) and **A shadow behind the text**, on by default, with its color, darkness, softness and distance.
- **How strong** is 50% by default. 0% shows nothing, and 100% is solid.
- **At the start** and **At the end** are the first and last 4 seconds, the same as notes.
- **Only during scenes on these backgrounds** shows with two or more backgrounds. It picks backgrounds, not scenes, since every performance has scenes of its own.
- A watermark fades in and out over less than half a second at the ends of its time.
- In a tall video, the corners are further in, clear of where a phone puts its own buttons.
- A watermark in the middle is drawn under the words, so it never covers them. Every other one is drawn over everything, the fades included.
- A picture is kept as a PNG, at most 512 pixels on its long side, so a logo keeps its see-through parts.
- Both are part of a saved look. **Start again** with **Settings** ticked takes the watermarks and their pictures out.

## Stickers

### In short

- A sticker is a picture of your own on the video, such as a drawing, a frame or a texture.
- Up to 20 stickers, each placed, sized and turned on the preview with the same dots as a shape.
- Each has an **Overlay type**, which changes how it mixes with what is under it, such as **Screen**, which makes black vanish.
- **Fill the frame** stretches one over the whole frame. **Layer** puts it over everything or behind the words.
- **When** shows it for the whole video, at the start, at the end, from a time, or during scenes on some backgrounds, with a short fade in and out.
- They are drawn in the preview, the example clip, full screen and every saved video. The downloaded audio has none.

### Steps

1. Open **Stickers** in the Studio and press **Add a sticker**.
2. Pick a PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF picture. It shows in the middle of the preview, with its long side 400 pixels of a 1080-pixel frame.
3. Drag it on the preview. The dot at a corner changes the size. The dot above turns it. The boxes under **Across**, **Down**, **Width**, **Height** and **Turn** set exact values.
4. Pick an **Overlay type**. The line under it says what that type does.
5. Pick **See-through**, **Layer** and **When**.
6. Look at the preview. Each change shows at once.

**Delete** takes a sticker out, and asks first.

### What it sends

Nothing. Stickers are drawn on your device. Each sticker's picture is kept on your account in a file of its own. See [Privacy](privacy.md).

### An example

Pell Orrin makes a video of a ghost story and wants it to look like an old photograph.

1. **Add a sticker** with a picture of dust and scratches, white on black.
2. **Fill the frame**, so it covers the whole video.
3. **Overlay type**: **Screen**. The black part vanishes, and only the white scratches show over the scene.
4. **See-through**: 60%, so the scratches are faint.
5. A second sticker, a small drawing of a candle with see-through parts. Dragged to the bottom left, with **Layer**: **Behind the words**, so the words are never covered.
6. **When**: **From a time, for a number of seconds**, from 12 for 8 seconds, for the part of the story where the candle is lit.

In the video, faint scratches run over every frame. The candle fades in at 12 seconds behind the words and fades out at 20.

### Details

- **Overlay type** is one of these. Each is the same as the blend mode of that name in a drawing program.

  | Overlay type | What it does |
  | --- | --- |
  | Normal | Drawn as it is. |
  | Multiply | Darkens: white parts vanish, dark parts stay. |
  | Screen | Lightens: black parts vanish, light parts stay. |
  | Overlay | Adds contrast: lights get lighter and darks darker. |
  | Soft light | A gentler Overlay. |
  | Lighten | Keeps whichever is lighter, the sticker or what is under it. |
  | Darken | Keeps whichever is darker, the sticker or what is under it. |
  | Color dodge | Brightens what is under it, strongly. |
  | Color burn | Darkens what is under it, strongly. |
  | Difference | Shows the difference in color, which can look inverted. |

- **Layer**: **Over everything** is drawn over the words, the pictures and the fades, and under the watermarks. **Behind the words** is drawn on the background, over its shapes.
- **Fill the frame** covers the whole frame, cut from the middle of the picture, so it keeps its shape. It cannot be dragged or sized while it is on. **Flip across** and **Flip up and down** still work.
- **See-through** is solid by default and goes to 95%.
- **When** has the same choices as a watermark. **At the start** and **At the end** are the first and last 4 seconds. A sticker fades in and out over less than half a second at the ends of its time.
- **Bring forward** and **Send back** change which sticker is on top. **Duplicate** makes a copy with a copy of its picture. **Lock** keeps it where it is, and **Lock all** at the top locks every sticker and shape.
- Stickers snap while they move, the same as shapes. See [Snapping](#snapping).
- A picture is kept at most 1600 pixels on its long side, as WebP where the browser can write it and PNG where it cannot, so its see-through parts stay.
- A GIF does not move in the video. Its first frame is used, and the panel says so when it is added.
- Stickers are part of a saved look and of a project. A deleted sticker's picture stays on your account while a saved look or a project still uses it, and is taken out once nothing does. **Start again** with **Settings** ticked takes every sticker and its picture out.
- Deleting a sticker cannot be undone, so **Undo** goes back no further than that.

## Hide and bleep words

- **Hide words**: off by default.
- **Words to hide**: one on each line. A word is hidden wherever it is said, with or without a final 's, and whatever marks are around it. An entry of two words, such as a full name, is hidden only where both are said together.
- **Add the cast's names** adds every name, display name and other name in the cast, except the narrator.
- **Cover them with**: **A black bar** by default, **Blur** or **Pixels**. It covers the words in the lines, the captions, the title and the names.
- **Bleep them in the audio**: on by default. A 1 kHz tone covers each hidden word, in the video and in downloaded audio.

The time of each word is worked out from where the voice is, so it is close, not exact. The bleep starts a little early and ends a little late, so it covers the whole word. It can catch the edge of the next word.
